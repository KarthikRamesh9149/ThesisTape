# Investment research memo

As of 2025-06-17

**Research question**
Given Statistics Canada's reports of a 0.2% GDP contraction in February 2025 and a subsequent 6.6% plunge in building permits, how has the outlook for Canadian domestic demand shifted? Analysis should contrast Q4 2024 growth against softening retail sales and the 7.0% May unemployment rate.

Some parts of the question could not be established from eligible evidence.

## Answer

As of 2025-06-17, I would flag downside risk to Canadian domestic demand but not downgrade the growth forecast from its Q4 2024 pace on this evidence alone. Statistics Canada’s tracker notice identifies relevant series but gives neither the quarterly growth benchmark nor the monthly readings. Holding the forecast is a decision not to act on unverified figures, not a finding that households and businesses sustained their earlier spending pace. ## Evidence

The contrast between Q4 2024 growth and subsequent household weakness is plausible but unverified. The notice lists retail sales without a reference month, direction or distinction between dollar sales and volume. It also does not verify the unemployment rate attributed to May. Even a confirmed May rate would need a prior reading to establish deterioration; a rate level by itself does not show that households cut spending. [1](https://web.archive.org/web/20250510235106/https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250410/dq250410e-eng.htm#:~:text=The%20Canadian%20Economic%20Tracker%20presents%20selected%20monthly%20indicators%20from,release%2C%20%22Canadian%20Economic%20Tracker%3A%20A%20new%20data%20visualization%20tool.%22)

## What would change the view

Test the unchanged forecast against quarterly real final domestic demand, comparing its growth with Q4 2024 on the same basis. The strongest case for a downgrade would be slower real consumption and investment, corroborated by sustained retail-volume weakness and rising unemployment in dated releases. If spending instead holds up, an isolated output dip and volatile permits would be a better explanation than a common demand slowdown. The tracker notice directs readers to the underlying analysis but does not resolve that test.
