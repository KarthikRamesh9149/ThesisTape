# Investment research memo

As of 2023-03-22

**Research question**
As of 22 March 2023, how did the Federal Reserve change its federal funds target range, and how did banking stress affect its policy guidance and the outlook for credit?

Some parts of the question could not be established from eligible evidence.

## Answer

On 2023-03-22, the Federal Open Market Committee raised its federal funds target range by 25 basis points. ## Outlook

The base case was tighter credit for households and businesses, not a demonstrated nationwide credit crunch. After the run at Silicon Valley Bank, other banks had reason to conserve cash by applying stricter loan standards. Even with the policy rate unchanged after March, reduced access to loans would tend to restrain spending, hiring and ultimately inflation. The direction was more predictable than the magnitude: the available evidence measures neither how broadly banks would tighten nor for how long, so it cannot translate the shock into an equivalent rate hike. [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1146126744) [4](https://web.archive.org/web/20230322220016/https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm#:~:text=The%20Federal%20Reserve%20controls%20the%20three%20tools%20of%20monetary,the%20Committee%27s%20assessment%20of%20the%20economy%20and%20policy%20options.)

## What would change the view

The strongest alternative was that depositor protection would contain the stress and leave lending outside the affected banks largely intact. Stable deposits, little change in reported lending standards and resilient lending alongside persistent inflation would strengthen the case for another hike. Broadly tighter standards and weaker lending, by contrast, would support waiting to see how much demand slowed. Protecting Silicon Valley Bank’s depositors was evidence for containment, not proof that other banks would keep lending on the same terms. [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1146126744) [5](https://web.archive.org/web/20230322220016/https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm#:~:text=The%20FOMC%20holds%20eight%20regularly%20scheduled%20meetings%20per%20year.,available%20online.%20Jerome%20H.%20Powell%2C%20Board%20of%20Governors%2C%20Chair)
