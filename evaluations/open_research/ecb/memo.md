# Investment research memo

As of 2024-06-07

**Research question**
As of 7 June 2024, what did the European Central Bank decide about its key interest rates, why did it act despite inflation risks, and what would guide its next decision?

## Answer

As of 2024-06-07, the ECB Governing Council had announced, but not yet implemented, a cut of 25 basis points in all three key rates. The decision on 6 June 2024 would take the deposit rate to 3.75%, the main refinancing rate to 4.25% and the marginal lending rate to 4.50% from 12 June 2024. After nine months on hold, the Council judged that disinflation justified less restraint, not an end to restrictive policy. [1](https://web.archive.org/web/20240606210424/https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/press_conference/monetary-policy-statement/shared/pdf/ecb.ds240606~f4d2c4833d.en.pdf?3d009dc9ec7917c832fe9a5a41e12fcd) [2](https://web.archive.org/web/20240607165426/https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ecb.mp240606%7E2148ecdb3c.en.html)

## Evidence

The case for cutting rested on more than the headline rate. Since September 2023, inflation had fallen by more than 2.5 percentage points. Underlying inflation had eased and inflation expectations had declined across horizons. The Council judged that its earlier tightening was working: restrictive financing conditions had weakened demand and helped keep expectations anchored. [2](https://web.archive.org/web/20240607165426/https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ecb.mp240606%7E2148ecdb3c.en.html)

Elevated wage growth was still keeping domestic price pressures high, giving the Governing Council reason to remain cautious even as it cut. June staff projections raised both headline and core inflation for 2024 and 2025 relative to March, but still showed annual headline inflation falling from 2.5% in 2024 to 2.2% in 2025 and 1.9% in 2026. The near-term forecast had worsened since March without reversing the disinflation achieved since September. The cut reflected that progress, not a conclusion that inflation was back at target. [1](https://web.archive.org/web/20240606210424/https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/press_conference/monetary-policy-statement/shared/pdf/ecb.ds240606~f4d2c4833d.en.pdf?3d009dc9ec7917c832fe9a5a41e12fcd) [3](https://web.archive.org/web/20240607145234/https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/projections/html/ecb.projections202403_ecbstaff~f2f2d34d5a.en.html)

## Outlook

A reasonable base case was a pause at the next meeting if wage and underlying price pressures persisted. The strongest alternative was another cut if underlying inflation eased further, incoming economic and financial data strengthened the outlook for a timely return to 2%, and financing conditions continued to restrain demand. That last test measures policy transmission: how strongly existing rates are affecting the economy. The Council would weigh all three considerations meeting by meeting; its statement established neither a trigger for another cut nor its timing. [2](https://web.archive.org/web/20240607165426/https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ecb.mp240606%7E2148ecdb3c.en.html)
