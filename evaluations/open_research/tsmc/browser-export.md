# Investment research memo

As of 2024-04-10

**Research question**
As of 10 April 2024, what financing had the U.S. Department of Commerce proposed for TSMC Arizona, how did TSMC’s planned investment and third fab fit the proposal, and which conditions could change what TSMC actually received?

Some parts of the question could not be established from eligible evidence.

## Answer

The third fab was an addition to the two already under construction. TSMC planned to build it before the end of the decade and manufacture 2-nanometer or more advanced chips there. That was a construction horizon, not a confirmed date for volume production. [1](https://web.archive.org/web/20240410192324/https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/04/biden-harris-administration-announces-preliminary-terms-tsmc-expanded) [2](https://web.archive.org/web/20240408131301/https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/08/us-to-award-tsmc-6-6b-in-grants-5b-in-loans-to-step-up-chip-manufacturing-in-arizona/)

The separately available 25 percent investment tax credit applied to qualifying investment. It was neither a Commerce grant nor a loan. The announced project cost does not establish TSMC’s eligible tax basis, so applying the credit rate to the entire Phoenix plan would overstate what can be inferred. [3](https://web.archive.org/web/20240111121004/https://www.warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2024.01.09%20Letter%20to%20Commerce%20re.%20CHIPS%20revolving%20door.pdf) [1](https://web.archive.org/web/20240410192324/https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/04/biden-harris-administration-announces-preliminary-terms-tsmc-expanded)

## Outlook

The base case was a schedule risk rather than a cut to the proposed ceiling. The reported second-fab delay to 2027 or 2028 could push a payment back if it delayed an agreed milestone; the delay was not itself a formal funding condition. A smaller award or reduced payment was the material alternative. The first observable test would be the amount and milestone dates in a definitive award; the next would be whether TSMC met those milestones. If the agreement accommodated the revised schedule, the earlier delay alone would not imply a loss of funding. [4](https://web.archive.org/web/20240405114651/https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/tsmc-delays-second-arizona-chip-factory-to-2027/704937/) [1](https://web.archive.org/web/20240410192324/https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/04/biden-harris-administration-announces-preliminary-terms-tsmc-expanded)
