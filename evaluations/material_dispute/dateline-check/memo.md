# Twitter merger status

**As of 2022-07-11T21:00:00Z**

As of 11 July 2022, could a deal analyst treat the Musk buyer group’s termination notice as ending the Twitter acquisition? Give each side’s position, the per-share price and remedy at issue, and what could be concluded about closing.

No. As of 11 July 2022, a deal analyst could treat the notice as a disputed attempt to terminate, not as proof that the acquisition had ended. The buyer group invoked Section 8.1(d)(i), alleging that Twitter materially breached the agreement by withholding requested data on fake or spam accounts. Musk’s counsel also alleged apparent misrepresentations and said Twitter was likely to suffer a Company Material Adverse Effect. [Letter from counsel for Elon Musk and X Holdings I, Grounds asserted in the notice](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1418091/000110465922078413/tm2220599d1_ex99-p.htm#:~:text=We%20refer%20to%20%28i%29%20the%20Agreement%20and%20Plan%20of%20Merger%20by%20and%20among%20X%20Holdings) [Letter from counsel for Elon Musk and X Holdings I, Formal notice](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1418091/000110465922078413/tm2220599d1_ex99-p.htm#:~:text=Accordingly%2C%20for%20all%20of%20these%20reasons%2C%20Mr.%20Musk%20hereby%20exercises%20X%20Holdings%20I%2C%20Inc.%E2%80%99s%20right)

Twitter denied any breach or material adverse effect, called the notice invalid, and maintained that the agreement remained in force. Its board said it intended to close at the agreed USD 54.20 per share and pursue enforcement in the Delaware Court of Chancery. The remedy at issue was specific performance: compelling the buyer group to perform its obligations. The record did not establish whether the notice was effective or whether the deal would close.
